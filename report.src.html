Five findings that change the picture

1. The property is in Clayton County — not Henry County Official

The "Hampton, GA 30228" address leads nearly everyone to assume Henry County. The U.S. Census geocoder returns Clayton County (FIPS 13063), the parcel is served by the Clayton County tax assessor, and the HOA describes itself as a Clayton County community. This determines zoning, permitting, property tax millage, and school assignment — all Clayton County, not Henry.

2. The lot does not touch the water Official GIS-derived

Listings market this as a "lakefront" lot with "direct access to the water." The county parcel polygon tells a different story: Lot 23's south boundary adjoins parcel 05045 051001, owned by the Clayton County Water Authority (CCWA) and appraised at $5.48M. The reservoir's edge lies roughly 59–80 ft inside CCWA land. Lot 23 contains zero water. It is lake-adjacent with a view, not riparian, and carries no apparent water frontage rights.

3. The lake is a public drinking-water reservoir — expect no private dock Official

The water body is J.W. Smith Reservoir, a CCWA public water-supply reservoir. CCWA sells access permits ($5 day / $25 annual), restricts hours, closes J.W. Smith entirely from November through February, and prohibits swimming and wading. A private dock would have to be built on and over land and water the buyer does not own, belonging to a public water utility. Treat dock, motorized boating and swimming as not available until CCWA says otherwise in writing.

4. The terrain is materially steeper than advertised GIS-derived

A 2021 listing called this a "perfect gentle rolling lot." USGS 3DEP lidar measures 80.2 ft of fall across the lot and an 18.3% average grade, reaching 23.5% over the steepest 50 ft. Independently, USDA maps the soil as the 10–25% slope phase with a representative slope of 18% — two unrelated sources agreeing. This is a moderate slope lot (locally steep), which drives foundation type, driveway design, retaining walls and cost.

5. Two Clayton County moratoriums are active right now Official

Resolution 2026-153 suspends acceptance of applications for short-term rentals (plus boarding/group/personal-care homes) through 31 December 2026. Resolution 2026-120 suspends applications for new single-family subdivision development on the same timeline. The STR moratorium directly contradicts any Airbnb/VRBO investment thesis for now; the development moratorium needs a direct answer from the county as to whether a single home on an existing platted lot is caught by it.

What this profile actually means

Good news for foundations: well drained, no shallow water table, and low shrink–swell. Expansive-clay heave — the classic foundation-cracking mechanism — is not the risk here, because kaolinitic Piedmont clays are relatively inert compared with the smectitic clays that cause trouble.

The catch: the B horizon from 5 to 36 inches is 47.5% clay. That is the layer every footing, trench and drainfield will sit in. It holds water, drains more slowly than the sandy surface suggests, and is slick when wet — which matters for trench stability on an 18% grade.

"Eroded" is a warning in the map unit name. Only 5 inches of topsoil remain at 2% organic matter, and below that organic matter collapses to 0.25%. Historic erosion has already stripped this hillside once. Expect to import topsoil for any lawn or garden, and expect aggressive erosion-control obligations during construction.

If no public water: ideal well depth Unverified

In the Georgia Piedmont, domestic wells draw from fractured crystalline bedrock beneath the saprolite, not from the soil profile. With paralithic rock at ~50 in here, a well would case through the weathered zone and seek water-bearing fractures below. Regional practice puts productive domestic wells in this setting commonly in the 150–400 ft range, with casing set well below the soil/rock interface and grouted to protect water quality. Shallow wells are a poor idea on this site specifically because the lot drains toward — and any septic field would sit above — a public water supply. These are regional norms, not a site prediction. A licensed Georgia well driller and the Georgia EPD well-construction standards govern. In practice, public water is very likely available (CCWA), which makes this question largely academic — confirm first.

Methodology

All geometry was computed in UTM Zone 16N / NAD83 (EPSG:26916) to avoid the area and distance distortion that arises from measuring in degrees. The parcel polygon was taken from the Clayton County Tax Assessor ArcGIS service and reprojected; area, side lengths and bearings were computed from that polygon. Elevations were sampled from the USGS 3DEP elevation service on a grid aligned to the lot's long axis (10 ft spacing, 3,149 points, 583 inside the boundary), with an independent 1 m centreline transect for the profile. Contours, slope statistics, drainage direction and the water plane were derived from that grid. The reservoir surface was identified as the modal flat elevation (244.17 m / 801.1 ft, 362 concordant samples). Climate was computed from a 30-year daily ERA5 record for the coordinate. Soil came from USDA-NRCS Soil Data Access by polygon intersection. Distances to amenities are straight-line (haversine) from the parcel centroid using OpenStreetMap coordinates.
